## Summary
AT 256 scores each desk hypothesis by how well the scan supports the clamps placed at their nominal desk poses; the ticket says to reuse the registrar's fitness, "not a new metric".
The build still uses that fitness, with two changes: the correspondence tolerance is 5 mm instead of the gate's 1 mm, and the fitness is multiplied by the share of the clamp's unobstructed pixels that stay visible.
Without the scaling, a hypothesis that puts the clamp mostly out of the camera view scored a perfect 1.0 from only 437 visible points (the true one had 8684), so the true desk turn tied with a wrong one.
Without the looser tolerance, a clamp 3 mm off its nominal pose, as in the existing pose provider test, would score near 0 under every hypothesis and the step would never decide on real scans.
Both are settings or a few lines inside one function, so changing them later costs minutes; no other ticket uses the score.
