## Summary
AT 264 teaches B1, the greedy two-robot planner, to split the 41 battery lid screws between both robots. In the battery run, the cell simulation rejected all 762 of B1's feasibility queries as invalid requests, for two reasons. B1 numbered every operation of a query alike (`10**6` for each, so all were `op_1000000`), and it put every step of the other robot that ended after this robot's clock into one query, so a query could hold two operations of one robot. The fix numbers the operations apart and sends only the steps that overlap the candidate in time, one step per robot in each query. With the fix, the simulation accepts the screw pairs and both robots screw (24 and 17). Without it, Robot 2 screws all 41. The fix also changes B1's plans for products without screw groups (the angle grinder) when the real simulation is connected: their concurrent steps now get a real answer instead of a rejection. Fast tests with stand-in simulations are unchanged.
