How should two robots fetch screws at the same time, when both screwing-in operations fetch from the one screw nest plate and the cell simulation refuses them together?
I recommend a screw supply slot per robot in the simulation's cell file (a slot may name the robots it serves), because it changes data, not the simulation's answers, and matches a cell with two nest plates.

## Background
A screwing-in operation starts at the supply slot of its screw type, fetches the screw and screws it in. The plan function takes the first slot whose screw type matches. Both cell files have one such slot, the H5 screw nest plate (AT 42, not measured yet). Before running two operations of different robots together, B1 asks the cell simulation's feasibility check. That check refuses a pair when the two robots' motions come within 5 cm of each other at overlapping times.

## Scenario
Battery, lid in place. B1 proposes Robot 1 on screw 41 and Robot 2 on screw 21 together. The simulation answers `collision: op_24:transit of robot_2 conflicts with op_4:transit of robot_1`: both robots move to the same nest plate. B1 then gives every screw to Robot 1, one after the other: 41 to 0, makespan 1030 s. Robot 2 alone reaches all 41 screws.

## Why this came up
"Done when" of AT 151 asks that the battery plan uses both robots for screwing. An earlier run split the screws 26/15, but only because the conflict check samples 8 poses per motion and missed the conflict. That is luck, not a result.

## How each answer shapes the road
| Option | What is built | Scenario result | Paper and records | Measurement | Size and risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A (recommended): a slot per robot | Supply slots get an optional list of robots; the plan function picks the slot of its robot; the simulation cell file gets a second H5 nest near Robot 2 | Both robots screw at the same time | The sim cell needs a second nest plate that the real cell does not have yet | Two-robot makespan, roughly halved | A field in the cell file and the supply slots (techstack only) |
| B: wait instead of refuse | The simulation's feasibility check reports a conflict that a wait can resolve as feasible with a sync point; B1 adds the wait | Robots alternate at the nest and screw in parallel | One nest plate, as on the cell | Makespan with waits at the nest | Subtree change upstream; changes what the feasibility check means (ADR-0010) |

What would make the recommendation wrong: the real cell will only ever have one nest plate, so the parallel result of option A would not carry over to the cell.
